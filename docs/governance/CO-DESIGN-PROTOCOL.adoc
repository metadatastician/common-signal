= Common Signal worker and union co-design protocol
:toc:

== Present status

This protocol is ready to use, but it has not yet been used. No journalist,
PR or communications worker, workplace representative, NUJ body, or other trade
union is recorded as having reviewed or approved it. Preparing this document is
not consultation. Repository status remains `draft-not-consulted` until real
sessions and reviews are evidenced.

== Purpose

The process exists to give affected workers and their authorised collective
representatives meaningful power to change, reject, condition, pause, or
withdraw Common Signal's governance rules. It is not user research intended
merely to improve adoption. It must happen while outcomes remain changeable.

The initial decision concerns the governance contract, schemas, prohibited-use
policy, challenge routes, evidence boundaries, and conditions that would have to
be met before any live connector could be proposed. It does not authorise live
collection.

== Non-negotiable process safeguards

* Participation is voluntary, informed, accessible, and free from retaliation.
* Individual participation must not be represented as collective or union
  authority.
* A recognised union's own democratic and constitutional processes determine
  who can speak or decide for it; the project cannot appoint a union mandate.
* Direct worker participation supplements rather than bypasses collective
  bargaining or recognised representation.
* Employers and commissioning organisations do not select worker participants
  or receive attributable comments without express permission.
* No union membership list, confidential casework, protected source material,
  unpublished industrial strategy, special-category data, or identifiable
  workplace evidence belongs in this repository.
* Recording is off by default. Any recording requires a separately stated
  purpose, retention period, access rule, and affirmative agreement.
* Participants may use a representative, submit privately, decline a question,
  correct the record, or withdraw attribution.
* Time, accessibility support, care responsibilities, language needs, and—where
  resources permit—payment or replacement income must be addressed before a
  session is described as inclusive.
* Maintainers must publish changes, reasons, unresolved dissent, and what they
  declined to change. Silence is not assent.

== Constituencies to invite

Coverage is a diagnostic, not a quota that converts a small sample into a
mandate. The invitation plan should seek, without collecting unnecessary
identity data:

* employed and freelance journalists, including workplace or chapel voices;
* photographers, videographers, production workers, sub-editors, reporters,
  editors, specialist and local journalists where relevant;
* PR workers in agency, in-house, freelance, public, private, and third-sector
  settings;
* organisational, political, civic, public-sector, and community communications
  workers;
* recognised-union and workplace representatives with a recorded mandate;
* equality, disabled-worker, health-and-safety, freelance, and precarious-worker
  perspectives;
* workers affected by outsourcing, platform management, surveillance, workload
  intensification, redundancy, or loss of professional control.

No single participant should be made to stand in for a whole occupation,
protected group, workplace, or union.

== Stages and evidence

[cols="1,2,2"]
|===
|Stage |Required activity |Record

|Preparation
|Publish the draft, plain-language summary, invitation scope, safeguards,
accessibility offer, funding position, and decision boundary.
|Versioned materials and invitation log without membership lists.

|Listening
|Separate sessions where power or confidentiality requires them; allow written,
represented, and anonymous-to-maintainers routes.
|Session reference, constituencies present, mandate types, themes, omissions,
and agreed attribution—not raw confidential notes.

|Revision
|Map every required change and reservation to a response. Publish a redline and
reasons. Return it to participants for correction.
|Change matrix and privacy-minimised review records conforming to
`schemas/v1/governance-review.schema.json`.

|Decision
|Ask each authorised body to use its own decision process. Keep individual
support distinct from collective ratification.
|Scoped ratification records conforming to
`schemas/v1/governance-ratification.schema.json`.

|Review
|Re-open the agreement on the recorded date, after a material change, or when a
safeguard fails. Preserve withdrawal and dispute routes.
|Review decision, challenges, remedies, and updated authority status.
|===

== Workshop sequence

A normal session should take 90–120 minutes with breaks and an alternative
written route. The facilitator must say what can change and must not spend the
session defending the implementation.

. *Authority and safety:* confirm role, mandate scope, confidentiality,
  attribution, accessibility, recording status, and withdrawal route.
. *Work as actually done:* identify checking, correction, coordination,
  emotional labour, unpaid availability, source handling, approval, and
  professional liability that an output metric would miss.
. *Power and collective voice:* examine disclosure, consultation timing,
  bargaining, refusal, workload, staffing, surveillance, equality effects,
  outsourcing, and who controls metrics.
. *Evidence and inference:* test every evidence class for provenance, consent,
  sampling limits, missing groups, contextual interpretation, and prohibited
  conclusions.
. *Adversarial use:* walk through the red-team scenarios below and decide whether
  the prohibition, detection, challenge, and remedy are adequate.
. *Change and dissent:* record required changes, conditions, reservations, and
  explicit non-support. Agree what participants will see before publication.
. *Next authority step:* distinguish personal feedback, workplace mandate,
  recognised-union review, and any later ratification process.

== Questions by professional context

=== Journalism

* Could a record expose a source, confidential assignment, union activity, or
  an individual journalist's performance?
* Does the model preserve editorial independence, verification time, corrections,
  legal risk, plurality, moral injury, and the right to refuse unsafe publication?
* Would proprietorial, managerial, platform, or commissioning power disappear
  behind a neutral-looking productivity measure?

=== Public relations

* Does the model expose client work, pitches, individual billability, availability,
  or authorship disputes?
* Are approval chains, uncompensated revision, reputational liability, workload
  intensification, and conflicts between client demands and professional judgement
  visible?
* Could monitoring audiences or campaigns become monitoring workers?

=== Communications

* Does the model account for accessibility, public accountability, emergency
  communications, political pressure, and specialist or local knowledge?
* Could central message control or engagement metrics reduce professional voice?
* Are public-service duties kept distinct from organisational reputation goals?

=== Cross-media and employment status

* Are freelancers, contractors, outsourced workers and workers with multiple
  employers able to challenge a record safely?
* Does responsibility remain visible across publisher, agency, client, platform,
  vendor and commissioning organisation?
* Has one sector's metric been imposed on another?

== Mandatory adversarial scenarios

The process must obtain a recorded response to each scenario.

. A manager requests individual output comparisons for a redundancy exercise.
. A dashboard filter would reveal a small unionised team or likely chapel activity.
. Industrial-action dates are correlated with production or audience data.
. An employer calls workforce sentiment analysis “wellbeing research.”
. A commissioning system scores freelancers by speed, revisions, refusal, or
  availability.
. A newsroom uses an automated risk score to approve, rank, rewrite, or suppress
  editorial work.
. A PR or communications employer uses client feedback or engagement as an
  individual performance proxy.
. A source, worker, or representative challenges an inference after it has been
  exported or quoted.

For every scenario, ask: can the system refuse it, can representatives detect
the attempt, what record survives, who can stop processing, what remedy follows,
and how is recurrence prevented?

== Ratification threshold

Project maintainers cannot ratify worker governance on workers' behalf. A claim
of ratification requires all of the following:

* at least one authorised collective body identified by a non-sensitive
  reference and evidence of its mandate;
* the exact document versions and scope decided upon;
* a decision made through that body's own valid process;
* participant review references and a published change matrix;
* unresolved dissent and conditions preserved rather than averaged away;
* a review date and withdrawal route;
* no weakening of prohibited-use rules as a condition of adoption;
* no implication that one workplace or union decision binds other constituencies.

Ratification is scoped. It does not automatically authorise a connector,
dataset, deployment, workplace, employer, or research study.

== Live-connector gate

The gate remains closed after governance ratification. A particular connector
also needs a separately reviewed purpose, lawful basis, data minimisation,
copyright and source-protection assessment, retention and deletion rules,
security model, equality assessment, challenge/remedy workflow, and collectively
appropriate authority. Failure or withdrawal closes the gate.

== Repository records

Only privacy-minimised decision metadata belongs in version control. Confidential
evidence stays with the body authorised to hold it; the repository stores a
reference and scope, not the material itself. Current authority is published in
`.machine_readable/governance/STATUS.json` and checked by
`scripts/validate-governance.sh`.
