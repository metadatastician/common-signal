= Common Signal: evidence discovery to intelligence for action
:toc:

== Status

This is the design and admission contract for the wider observatory. Source
candidates have been identified, but no live connector or field-submission
channel is implemented or authorised. The current service remains synthetic and
read-only. `sources/registry.json` is an inventory, not evidence that a source
has been collected.

== Intended outcome

Common Signal should help affected workers, workplace and union representatives,
journalists, researchers, and public-interest organisations turn scattered
material into evidence they can inspect and use to:

* investigate a technology, employer claim, market development, or policy change;
* prepare questions for information and consultation;
* support collective negotiation without exposing membership or strategy;
* challenge unsafe monitoring, workload intensification, deskilling, or rights
  infringement;
* report accurately on structural changes in journalism, PR, communications,
  and the media industries;
* identify what is still unknown and what evidence would change a conclusion.

“Intelligence” here means a versioned, source-linked, contestable action brief.
It is not secret intelligence about workers, a prediction about individuals, or
an automated instruction.

== Surveillance and reconnaissance boundary

The word _surveillance_ is too dangerous to leave unqualified.

Common Signal may implement transparent *source and system watches* for:

* source availability, update times, API health, and schema drift;
* newly published official statistics, regulator guidance, union policy, public
  company filings, and public consultations;
* coverage gaps and changes in the set of published media sources indexed;
* public claims made by employers, vendors, publishers, platforms, regulators,
  and governments;
* worker-led documentation of workplace surveillance systems, when voluntarily
  submitted through a protected and collectively governed route.

It may perform *reconnaissance* to learn which lawful public sources exist, what
their coverage is, whether their licence permits the proposed processing, and
which questions they can and cannot answer.

It must not conduct or facilitate:

* covert or identifiable monitoring of workers, freelances, representatives, or
  sources;
* inference of union membership, organising, chapel activity, industrial-action
  preparation, or workforce/union sentiment;
* individual output, refusal, availability, correction, byline, engagement, or
  client-feedback scoring;
* deanonymisation of protected or confidential sources;
* automated decisions about discipline, dismissal, redundancy, work allocation,
  commissioning, editorial approval, or professional standing.

The TUC documents the risks of workplace surveillance and argues for worker and
union voice before monitoring is introduced; the ICO also treats monitoring of
workers and communications with representatives as carrying significant data
protection concerns. See
https://www.tuc.org.uk/research-analysis/reports/ill-be-watching-you[TUC,
_I'll be watching you_] and
https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/employment/monitoring-workers/data-protection-and-monitoring-workers/[ICO,
_Data protection and monitoring workers_].

== Source families

=== Worker and union authority

The NUJ's published AI resources provide a primary union-policy baseline on
human-centred journalism, consultation, copyright, jobs, skills, transparency,
source integrity, and professional control. They do not establish that every
member holds one view. Each motion, briefing, survey, or campaign item must
retain its authorising body, date, scope, and methodology. See
https://www.nuj.org.uk/resource/artificial-intelligence.html[NUJ,
_Artificial intelligence: journalism before algorithms_] and
https://www.nuj.org.uk/resource/dm2025-artificial-intelligence.html[NUJ Delegate
Meeting 2025 AI decisions].

TUC publications provide cross-sector evidence and proposals concerning worker
voice, algorithmic management, data reciprocity, surveillance, equality, and
collective bargaining. A policy report, consultation response, and survey are
different evidence classes and must remain distinguishable. See
https://www.tuc.org.uk/research-analysis/reports/dignity-work-and-ai-revolution[TUC,
_Dignity at work and the AI revolution_] and
https://www.tuc.org.uk/research-analysis/reports/ai-bill-project[TUC AI Bill
Project].

=== Official statistics and media research

The ONS API is a candidate for denominators and contextual series concerning
employment, occupation, industry, business conditions, and technology adoption.
The official developer documentation describes an unrestricted beta HTTP API
that supports dataset discovery and filtering. Beta status, revisions,
classification boundaries, dimensions, weighting, and disclosure controls must
travel with every result. See https://developer.ons.gov.uk/[ONS Developer Hub].

Ofcom's News Consumption and Media Nations research can provide audience,
platform, plurality, and market context. Audience consumption does not establish
working conditions or the causal effect of AI. Every extracted figure must keep
its wave, population, base, wording, method, and weighting. See
https://www.ofcom.org.uk/research-and-data[Ofcom research and data].

=== Regulation, rights, ownership, and accountability

ICO guidance is a change-watch source for worker monitoring, automated
decision-making, employment records, objections, and data rights. It is
regulatory guidance, not individual legal advice, and its own page currently
says parts are under review following legislative change.

The Companies House API is a candidate for organisation identity, status,
filings, and limited ownership context. The official API requires credentials.
The first proposed connector must exclude officer/person-level enrichment and
store credentials outside source control. Public filing data alone cannot prove
editorial control, employment practice, or beneficial influence. See
https://developer.company-information.service.gov.uk/[Companies House Developer
Hub].

Court and tribunal material is not automatically available for bulk intelligence
work merely because judgments are public. Find Case Law says computational
analysis requires separate licensing and prohibits external indexing without
permission. The candidate is therefore blocked pending written permission,
privacy and necessity assessment, and minimisation. See
https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/legal-framework[The National Archives,
Find Case Law legal framework] and
https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/terms-of-use[Find Case Law terms].

=== Published-media discovery

Media Cloud and GDELT may help find and compare published coverage. Neither
index is “the media,” and absence from either is not absence from reporting.
Coverage, source selection, language handling, translation, clustering, query
design, publisher rights, and changing APIs all affect results. Automated tone
must not be re-labelled public, workforce, or union sentiment. See
https://www.mediacloud.org/documentation[Media Cloud documentation] and
https://blog.gdeltproject.org/gdelt-doc-2-0-api-debuts/[GDELT DOC 2.0 API].

== Processing pipeline

[cols="1,2,2,2"]
|===
|Stage |Input |Required receipt |Failure behaviour

|Question and action horizon
|A worker-, union-, journalism-, or public-interest question
|Who needs what decision, by when; permitted purpose; prohibited-use review;
what result could change the proposed action
|Reject vague “monitor everything” briefs

|Source discovery
|Registry and transparent reconnaissance
|Candidate URL/API, authority, expected coverage, access method, licence state,
known blind spots
|Keep as candidate; do not collect

|Admission
|Source-specific proposal
|Governance, legality, licence, copyright, privacy, source protection, security,
retention, deletion, and connector-gate decision
|Reject or quarantine; purpose never overrides prohibited risk

|Acquisition
|Approved, scoped source
|Retrieval time, source version, query, pagination horizon, response status,
content digest, and raw-location reference
|No silent partial result; record paging/rate-limit failure

|Normalisation
|Immutable acquired item
|Original field, normalised field, transformation version, language, time zone,
classification map, exclusions
|Quarantine unparseable or schema-drifted records

|Deduplication and entity resolution
|Normalised candidates
|Exact/near-duplicate links, organisation identifiers, ambiguity and human
resolution
|Keep ambiguous entities separate

|Classification
|Evidence with provenance
|Evidence class, professional context, labour impact, source authority,
inference boundary, and prohibited-use decision
|Never collapse unlike evidence into one score

|Corroboration and contradiction
|Claims from one or more evidence classes
|Independent support, dependency between sources, contradicting evidence,
missing constituencies, and falsifiers
|Label single-source, contested, or inconclusive

|Field correction
|Voluntary protected submission or authorised collective input
|Consent scope, mandate, external evidence reference, withdrawal route,
retaliation/source risk, and participant correction
|Quarantine by default; raw evidence never goes in Git

|Action brief
|Reviewed claims and receipts
|Findings, evidence references, state, limitations, dissent, possible actions,
human decision owner, and review date
|No automatic adverse or editorial decision

|Outcome learning
|Human decision and subsequent evidence
|Action taken, authority, observed outcome, challenges, corrections, and whether
the original inference held
|Do not rewrite the historical receipt
|===

== Accuracy model

Common Signal cannot guarantee a universally “accurate dataset.” Accuracy is
claim-relative and bounded by instruments, sources, dates, definitions, and
missing populations. The system can instead make every material claim
auditable:

* enumerate the source horizon and pagination denominator;
* validate captured shapes, identifiers, dates, and units;
* retain source version, query, retrieval time, digest, and transformation;
* preserve corrections and source dependencies;
* distinguish absence from “not observed by this instrument”;
* require an explicit inference boundary and plausible falsifier;
* use `emerging`, `corroborated`, `contested`, or `inconclusive`, never an opaque
  universal confidence score;
* make human review, dissent, expiry, and re-review visible.

== Field input architecture

The future intake path should be a protected submission service, not a public
GitHub issue or ordinary analytics form. It requires:

* HTTPS and authenticated or represented routes appropriate to the risk;
* separation of identity/contact material from analytical content;
* pseudonymous references and encrypted, access-controlled storage;
* a route mediated by a recognised representative where requested;
* no collection of membership lists or organising strategy;
* explicit purpose, consent scope, retention, deletion, correction, withdrawal,
  and breach response;
* safe handling of legal privilege, source confidentiality, whistleblowing,
  safeguarding, and retaliation risk;
* optional offline or in-person routes for people who cannot safely use an
  employer-controlled device;
* publication only of minimised, reviewed claims with participant correction and
  an inference boundary.

`schemas/v1/field-observation.schema.json` describes public decision metadata,
not the raw testimony store.

== First connector order

. Manual, versioned NUJ/TUC/ICO policy baseline.
. Read-only ONS dataset catalogue and metadata reconnaissance—no bulk
  observations until a named question selects dimensions and denominators.
. Ofcom table inventory and methodology capture.
. Organisation-only Companies House prototype using a separately managed test
  credential and no person-level enrichment.
. Media Cloud/GDELT discovery experiment using a synthetic query corpus and
  measured coverage gaps, without retaining article bodies.
. Field submissions only after the governance, security, safeguarding,
  retention, deletion, and representation process is ratified and tested.

Court/tribunal computational analysis remains outside this order until its
licensing and privacy blockers are resolved.
