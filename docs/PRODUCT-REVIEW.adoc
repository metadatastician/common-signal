= Product review of the Common Signal preview
:toc:

== Observation horizon

This review covers the single local file supplied as
`/mnt/c/Users/USER/Downloads/preview.html`: 108,187 bytes and 1,551 lines,
inspected as source and as text rendered by `w3m`. A graphical browser automation
helper was unavailable, so this review does not claim pixel-level visual QA or
successful interaction in Chromium, Firefox, or assistive technology.

== What the preview is

The preview is an unusually complete executable product proposition for an
“AI and communicative life observatory.” It is a self-contained, offline HTML
application with synthetic fixtures, deterministic local filtering, display and
privacy settings, keyboard support, evidence-passport views, a work-system
view, a protocol registry, exports, and self-diagnostics.

It is not a live observatory. Its own copy is appropriately candid: it makes no
claim about real people or current events, denies network access in its Content
Security Policy, calls the data synthetic, and describes itself as an
architecture sketch rather than completed proof.

== What it is trying to do

The core intellectual move is to replace shallow “AI productivity” and
“sentiment” dashboards with a mechanism-and-evidence model. It asks who gains
capacity, who inherits verification and coordination burdens, how attention and
voice are redistributed, and where an inference must stop.

Six sections express that proposition:

* *Briefing* gives an editorially legible weekly account rather than a scorecard.
* *Pulse field* explores sampled public expressions while warning that
  expressions are not people and samples are not populations.
* *Explore* starts with a public question rather than a wall of measures.
* *Work & power* follows task displacement, judgement, responsibility, and
  control through a production workflow.
* *Methods* attaches provenance, uncertainty, limitations, and challenge status
  to claims.
* *System* makes schemas, transformations, adapters, diagnostics, and future
  proof obligations inspectable.

== What is strongest

=== Epistemic honesty

The page repeatedly distinguishes observation, classification, and inference.
It refuses to equate sampled expression with population feeling and it prevents
source classes from collapsing into a single index. This should remain a product
invariant, not merely explanatory copy.

=== Work is treated as a system

The preview sees that a faster first draft can create more checking, escalation,
correction, coordination, monitoring, and responsibility elsewhere. This is the
right basis for a union-centred product because it measures redistribution of
work and power, not only task duration.

=== Claims carry evidence passports

Scope, collection method, unit, sample, confidence, verification date,
limitations, and challenge status travel with each claim. That model can support
union scrutiny, corrections, and bargaining evidence far better than detached
dashboard annotations.

=== Privacy and accessibility are designed in

The prototype is offline, denies network connections, makes local learning
opt-in, does not store searches, has a skip link and named controls, supports
keyboard navigation, provides text alternatives to visual summaries, and
respects motion preferences. Those features fit the public-interest premise.

== What needs to change for the clarified mission

=== Labour and collective power must become explicit

The preview discusses workers and control but mostly at an individual workflow
level. Common Signal needs first-class representation for employment status,
freelance conditions, collective consultation, recognition and bargaining,
worker-elected governance, grievance and remedy, redundancy or role change,
pay, hours, surveillance, discrimination, health and safety, and the right to
refuse unsafe or unethical automation.

=== Journalism, PR, and communications require distinct contexts

These occupations overlap but do not share identical duties, power relations,
codes, legal risks, or measures of public value. The domain model must preserve
their differences while still supporting cross-media analysis. PR and comms
workers should not appear only as sources of influence on journalism; they are
workers with their own professional judgement, workloads, monitoring, and
collective interests.

=== Union participation must govern, not decorate

A “union lens” applied after collection would be insufficient. Worker and union
representatives should help decide what may be collected, for what purpose, how
small groups are protected, which comparisons are harmful, how long records are
retained, and what remedy follows a challenge. Workplace data must not be
repurposed for individual performance management, discipline, union
surveillance, or strike monitoring.

=== The Pulse field is the riskiest surface

The visual metaphor is compelling but may give emotion classifications more
authority than the warnings can undo. Any production version needs strong
provenance, multilingual and dialect audits, contestable labels, deletion and
appeal paths, small-group suppression, and a clear reason for including emotion
classification at all. It should never be used to infer the mood or legitimacy
of a workforce, union, membership, audience, or public.

=== A synthetic demo needs a migration boundary

The prototype currently combines authored fixtures, UI state, schemas, and
diagnostics in one HTML file. Production work should preserve it as a design
reference while moving meaning into versioned schemas and a canonical query
algebra. Live connectors must remain disabled until consent, lawful basis,
copyright, retention, worker-governance, and security obligations are specified
and tested.

== Recommended product definition

Common Signal should be a worker-governed evidence infrastructure and public
observatory for examining AI-mediated change in journalism, PR, communications,
and media work. It should support three related but non-interchangeable uses:

. *Public-interest understanding*: careful briefings on voice, plurality,
  information quality, and communicative power.
. *Workplace and bargaining understanding*: inspectable evidence about workload,
  control, independence, skills, surveillance, attribution, and distribution of
  benefit and risk.
. *Accountability and challenge*: provenance, limitations, corrections, worker
  consultation records, and routes to contest claims or data use.

It should not become an employee-scoring product, a newsroom productivity
leaderboard, a union or public sentiment detector, an automated editorial
decision-maker, or a mechanism for replacing consultation and bargaining.

== Assessment

The preview is conceptually strong, unusually honest, and worth developing. Its
best idea is not the animated field or any single dashboard; it is that every
claim about AI and communication must expose the work system, power shift,
evidence route, and inference boundary behind it. The clarified NUJ and union
movement focus makes that idea sharper. The next challenge is institutional:
worker governance and collective remedy must be embedded as deeply as the
technical provenance model.
